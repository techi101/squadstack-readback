Critical-slot QA for Indian phone voice agents

Find the turns where your agent acts on a misheard number.

Give it what the caller said and what the recogniser heard. Readback pulls out the slots that change what the agent does next (amounts, dates, a “नहीं”, a promise to pay, a request for a human), flags every one the recogniser got wrong, and shows what each confirm-back policy would catch and what it costs in talk time.

Runs in your browser. Nothing is uploaded. No API key needed Devanagari + romanised Hinglish + English

How it works

  1. 01

    Read the slots

    Each turn is normalised (nukta, ँ/ं, ₹, commas) and scanned for numbers in Devanagari, romanised Hindi and English, including साढ़े, सवा, पौने, डेढ़, ढाई and “छह हजार नौ सौ” style compounds; the caller’s yes/no answer and negations; payment promises, “already paid” and refusals; and handoff, stop-calling and distress phrases.

  2. 02

    Diff said vs heard

    Slots from the reference are matched to slots from the recogniser output. A number can be changed, dropped or inserted; a “no” can be lost or heard as “yes”; a promise can be lost, invented or flipped. Word-level alignment is shown for context only.

  3. 03

    Simulate readback

    A policy may only use what the agent heard. A wrong value it heard gets read back and the caller can correct it; a value the recogniser dropped is invisible to a slot readback. Talk-time cost = readbacks × your seconds-per-readback.

  4. 04

    Decide per campaign

    Filter by campaign to see where readback pays for itself. A BFSI reminder with many amounts and dates may want “Numbers”; a lead-qualification call may not.

Honest limits

  • The sample is synthetic. 20 short calls written by hand with errors inserted on purpose, modelled on error types SquadStack describes publicly. The counts on this page describe that sample only, not any real recogniser or SquadStack’s agents.
  • Rules, not a model. Slot extraction is word lists and patterns. It will miss paraphrases and rarer spellings; romanised “saath” (60) is skipped because it usually means “with”, and “do / दो” counts as 2 only next to another number word.
  • Turns must be aligned. Said and heard text are compared turn by turn, as in the public benchmark’s normalised columns.
  • Readback is simulated with a cooperative caller who always corrects a wrong value. Real callers sometimes say “haan” to anything.
  • Benchmark import is untested on real rows. The parser follows the public dataset card of the SquadStack Conversational Streaming ASR Benchmark. The data is gated, and the author has not used it.
